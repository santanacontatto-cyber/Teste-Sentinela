# Sentinela — Narrative Watch

Public, timestamped investigation of how AI-safety events, narratives, institutions and policy proposals interact.

## Why this exists

The question is **not** whether AI risk is real or fake. The current evidence supports that some technical risks are real. The question is narrower and testable:

> When a real AI event becomes a focusing event, do public interpretation and policy response converge on solutions that were already prepared by a relatively small overlapping ecosystem of labs, forecasters, evaluators, funders and policy actors?

This repository is a public trail created **before the next major event** so later explanations cannot be rewritten to fit whatever happens.

## Rules

1. Separate **fact**, **inference**, **prediction**, and **speculation**.
2. Never treat shared funding, shared personnel or agreement as proof of coordination.
3. Never treat a real technical incident as proof that a specific policy response is necessary.
4. Record predictions before checking the outcome.
5. Keep failed predictions. Do not delete or silently edit them.
6. Every prediction needs a disconfirmation condition.
7. Update by dated commits. Git history is part of the evidence.
8. Prefer primary sources; label secondary reporting clearly.

## Current working model

The model under test is:

**prepared frame → real focusing event → amplification → policy window → concrete institutional response**

This is **not** a claim that the event was fabricated. It is a claim about how real events may be interpreted and converted into policy.

## What would change my mind

The working model weakens if future major incidents repeatedly produce policy responses that were **not** part of the prepared menu, if incumbent labs support measures that materially disadvantage themselves without seeking carve-outs, or if the apparent network overlap fails to predict who gains influence after a focusing event.

The point is not to win the hypothesis. The point is to leave a trail that can prove it wrong.
