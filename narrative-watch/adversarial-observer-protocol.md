# Adversarial Observer Protocol

Narrative Watch is public. That creates a methodological problem: once observed actors can see the predictions, their later behavior may be influenced by the observation itself.

This protocol treats that as a first-class failure mode rather than pretending the system under study is passive.

## 1. Two evidence lanes

**Public lane:** explicit, falsifiable predictions that anyone can inspect in advance.

**Sealed lane:** holdout predictions whose exact text remains private until a predefined reveal condition. Only a cryptographic commitment is public in advance.

Agreement between the two lanes is stronger than agreement inside one lane alone. Disagreement must remain visible.

## 2. Contamination

A prediction is `CONTAMINATED` when there is evidence that an observed actor encountered Narrative Watch or was responding to it before the prediction resolved.

Contaminated results are not counted as clean confirmation or falsification of natural behavior. The contamination itself can be studied separately.

## 3. No intent from surface correlation

The following are insufficient by themselves to establish deliberate coordination or manipulation:

- similar wording;
- common funders;
- simultaneous publication;
- participation in the same professional network;
- benefiting from the same policy;
- social-media volume;
- one actor privately or publicly agreeing with another.

Claims of deliberate coordination require additional evidence such as documented communication, contracting, financial direction, explicit campaign planning, attributable instructions, or a reproducible sequence that strongly excludes plausible independent convergence.

## 4. Paid amplification / astroturfing check

A surge in apparent support is not treated as independent corroboration until independence is examined.

Where evidence is available, record:

- funding and sponsorship disclosures;
- lobbying registrations and expenditures;
- advertising libraries;
- PR or communications firms;
- repeated text or synchronized publication patterns;
- organizational or personnel overlap;
- whether sources ultimately trace back to the same primary claim;
- whether supposedly independent experts have disclosed financial or institutional ties.

Absence of discovered ties is not proof of independence.

## 5. Actor-cost test

When a proposal is justified as safety policy, record its actual distribution of costs and powers:

- who pays fixed compliance costs;
- who receives regulatory access or exemptions;
- whether proponents accept the same restriction themselves;
- whether the rule is symmetric across competitors;
- whether national-security or competitive exceptions weaken the stated safety principle;
- whether smaller or open actors can practically comply.

A policy that harms its proponent materially is evidence against a simple self-interest account, but does not prove altruistic motive.

## 6. Counterfactual discipline

For every apparent hit, ask what else should be observed if the preferred explanation were false.

Examples:

- If the event is only a technical defect, do responses stay focused on technical remediation?
- If policy reuse is ordinary preparedness rather than capture, do actors also support measures that impose substantial symmetric costs on themselves?
- If narrative convergence is independent, do distinct actors arrive through distinct evidence and reasoning rather than a common source chain?

## 7. No moving the target

Each prediction resolves as one of:

- `HIT`
- `MISS`
- `UNRESOLVED`
- `CONTAMINATED`
- `METHOD FAILURE`

No category may be redefined after the evidence arrives merely to preserve the hypothesis.

## 8. Distribution strategy

Do not maximize reach at the expense of the experiment.

Before sealed holdouts resolve, prefer distribution to independent observers, methodologists, journalists or researchers who are not themselves central actors in the prediction. Directly alerting the actors being observed is treated as a potential intervention and logged as such.

## 9. Strong claim threshold

Narrative Watch may document policy windows, coordinated amplification, institutional overlap, incentive alignment or regulatory capture mechanisms without claiming a hidden conspiracy.

A claim that incidents were fabricated, induced, staged or knowingly misrepresented requires direct evidence substantially stronger than pattern matching.

The project is allowed to become less interesting. If the evidence points to ordinary safety work, that result stays.
