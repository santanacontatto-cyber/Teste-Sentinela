# Narrative Watch — Costly integrity boundary test

Date frozen: 2026-09-13

This record tests a refinement of the prior **costly opportunism** hypothesis.

The relevant question is no longer whether an actor ever accepts a cost. Some actors clearly do. The stronger question is:

> **Which constraints survive when the actor is forced to choose between the constraint and a material objective it strongly wants?**

A single costly act does not prove purity of motive, and a later policy relaxation does not prove the original principle was fake. The historical pattern may instead be **selective costly integrity**: some boundaries survive large costs while other constraints migrate when strategic conditions change.

---

## 1. Anthropic–Pentagon dispute: strongest costly-integrity case found so far

### Constraint before the cost

Anthropic stated that it would support lawful national-security uses of Claude except for two narrow categories: mass domestic surveillance of Americans and fully autonomous weapons.

Primary sources:
- https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-department-of-war
- https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-comments-secretary-war

### External pressure

The U.S. Department of War demanded removal of those safeguards and threatened Anthropic with exclusion and a supply-chain-risk designation.

Anthropic did not yield. It publicly stated that the threats did not change its position and that, if necessary, it would accept offboarding from the Department rather than remove the two safeguards.

### Material cost

Court records later described concrete business damage. Before the dispute, Anthropic projected several hundred million dollars in 2026 public-sector annual recurring revenue and billions over five years. After the challenged government actions, deals worth hundreds of millions of dollars were delayed, prospective customers withdrew, and some customers terminated contracts.

Court record:
- https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3%3A2026cv01996/465515/134/

Reuters also reported that the designation threatened billions in projected revenue:
- https://www.reuters.com/world/how-anthropic-pentagon-dispute-over-ai-safeguards-escalated-2026-03-11/

### Outcome

On 2026-08-28, a federal judge ruled the Pentagon's blacklisting unlawful, finding unlawful retaliation, due-process violations and arbitrary/capricious action.

Source:
- https://www.reuters.com/world/us-judge-rules-pentagon-blacklisting-anthropic-unlawful-2026-08-28/

### Classification

**Strong counterexample to a pure-opportunism model.**

The cost was material, immediate and not guaranteed to be recovered. Reputation and differentiation may have created benefits, but there is no evidence that the financial and government-access damage was staged or fully compensated ex ante.

This case shows that at least one frontier lab can preserve a narrow principle while absorbing a serious unilateral cost.

## 2. Anthropic and China-linked customers: second costly case, but geopolitically aligned

Anthropic says it voluntarily forgave several hundred million dollars in revenue by cutting off firms linked to the Chinese Communist Party and has advocated stronger chip export controls.

Primary source:
- https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-department-of-war

This is real self-cost if the revenue estimate is accurate, but the action also aligns with Anthropic's stated U.S./democratic national-security strategy. Therefore it is evidence of costly consistency, but a weaker test of motive than the Pentagon case because principle and geopolitical positioning point in the same direction.

Classification: **costly consistency with strategic alignment.**

## 3. Google Project Maven: costly integrity existed, then the boundary later migrated

In 2018 Google stated that it would not pursue follow-on Project Maven contracts and formalized AI Principles prohibiting AI weapons whose principal purpose was causing injury. Google explicitly acknowledged that this meant not pursuing certain government contracts.

Primary sources:
- https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/incorporating-googles-ai-principles-google-cloud/
- https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/ai-principles/

This followed large employee protests and resignations, so the decision also served internal legitimacy and retention. Still, Google accepted the lost follow-on work and created a category-level prohibition.

In 2025 Google removed the categorical no-weapons list and replaced it with a broader principles framework emphasizing benefit/risk, international law, security and democratic AI leadership.

Historical significance: **the original costly principle need not have been fake for the later migration to be real.**

Classification: **costly integrity followed by later constraint migration.**

## 4. OpenAI Astra delay: real cost, but reversible and bounded

OpenAI states that, after Astra reached its Critical cybersecurity threshold, it delayed parts of development and release while strengthening safeguards. It also temporarily slowed scaling.

Primary sources:
- https://openai.com/index/pacing-model-development-cyber-capabilities/
- https://openai.com/index/path-to-astra/

Astra was then released on 2026-09-03 after OpenAI said safeguards were sufficient.

Primary source:
- https://openai.com/index/safety-overview-gpt-6-astra/

Classification: **real but limited costly signal.**

It demonstrates that a safety framework can delay capability work, but the delay was measured in weeks and preserved the ultimate release option. It is not comparable in strength to losing government access or hundreds of millions in business.

---

## 5. Meta military-use shift: useful opposite case

In 2024 Reuters reported that Meta's acceptable-use rules prohibited military and espionage applications. Days later Meta announced that Llama would be made available to U.S. government agencies and contractors for defense and national-security uses, while framing the move as responsible support for U.S. leadership and global security.

Sources:
- https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/chinese-researchers-develop-ai-model-military-use-back-metas-llama-2024-11-01/
- https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/open-source-ai-america-global-security/

Classification: **constraint migration under geopolitical distinction.**

This does not show that Meta's earlier restriction was fraudulent. It shows that a broad prohibition was rapidly narrowed once the beneficiary and geopolitical context changed.

# Result

The strongest model supported by the evidence so far is **not**:

- 'all safety claims are fake', or
- 'costly action proves sincere principle'.

It is:

## Selective Costly Integrity + Constraint Migration

1. Organizations can hold some principles strongly enough to absorb large unilateral costs.
2. The same organization can relax or replace other constraints when strategic, competitive or geopolitical conditions change.
3. Therefore sincerity and opportunism are not opposites. They can coexist inside the same institution.
4. The central empirical variable is **which boundaries survive which kinds of cost**.

This reframes the investigation. Instead of assigning one motive to an organization, Narrative Watch should estimate a **constraint-survival function**.

---

## Variables to track next

For every hard constraint:

1. **Specificity** — is the principle narrow and concrete or broad and aspirational?
2. **Identity centrality** — is it part of the organization's public identity?
3. **Cost magnitude** — actual money, compute, contracts, release delay, access or strategic position lost.
4. **Reversibility** — can the decision be undone cheaply later?
5. **Who bears the cost** — the actor alone, rivals equally, customers, government or the public?
6. **Compensation** — reputation, regulatory advantage, market differentiation, legal protection or government favor gained.
7. **External enforcement** — is compliance independently auditable or self-certified?
8. **Geopolitical alignment** — does the principle also advance the actor's national or strategic interest?
9. **Persistence** — does the constraint still hold months or years later after leadership and market conditions change?

The most informative case is not a costly announcement. It is a constraint that remains in force **after repeated opportunities to relax it become materially attractive**.

---

## Falsification rule

The broader opportunism hypothesis must be weakened if multiple frontier institutions repeatedly preserve pre-existing hard constraints through large, unilateral, persistent and weakly compensated costs.

Conversely, the constraint-migration hypothesis strengthens if hard constraints systematically become discretionary when the cost becomes unilateral, strategically inconvenient or persistent.

Neither result requires inferring secret intent.
