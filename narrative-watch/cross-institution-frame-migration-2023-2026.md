# Narrative Watch — Cross-institution frame migration audit (2023–2026)

Date frozen: 2026-09-13

This audit extends the earlier cross-lab work beyond companies. It asks whether a similar structural mechanism appears in governments, regulators, philanthropic networks, policy organizations, researchers and journalism:

> When an institution faces rising strategic, political, financial or geopolitical pressure, does a previously hard safety frame remain hard, or does the institution preserve the high-level value while changing the mechanism, vocabulary or enforcement architecture?

This is not an accusation of conspiracy. Shared funding, shared language, shared personnel or timing are not treated as proof of central coordination.

---

## 1. United Kingdom — from AI Safety to AI Security + growth

### 2023 baseline

The Bletchley Declaration and the inaugural AI Safety Summit explicitly centered frontier-AI risks, including potentially serious or catastrophic harm, misuse, loss-of-control concerns, safety testing, evaluations, transparency and international scientific cooperation.

Primary sources:
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-the-bletchley-declaration/the-bletchley-declaration-by-countries-attending-the-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-chairs-statement-state-of-the-science-2-november

### 2025 change

On 14 February 2025, the UK renamed the AI Safety Institute the **AI Security Institute**. The government said the new framing would focus on national-security and crime risks such as chemical/biological misuse, cyberattacks, fraud and child sexual abuse. The same announcement tied the reset to economic growth and highlighted a new agreement with Anthropic on government opportunities and productivity.

Primary source:
- https://www.gov.uk/government/news/tackling-ai-security-risks-to-unleash-growth-and-deliver-plan-for-change

### Classification

**Frame migration — strong.** The institution did not abandon risk work, but the center of gravity moved from a broad frontier-safety frame toward security, criminal misuse, government capability and growth.

This does **not** prove safety was weakened. It does show that the meaning of the institutional mission became more compatible with national-security and economic-state interests.

## 2. United States — explicit regime-level reversal

### 2023 baseline

Executive Order 14110 made "safe, secure, and trustworthy" AI governance a federal priority. It required standardized evaluations, risk-management mechanisms, reporting and testing for some advanced systems and directed the creation of safety institutions and standards.

Primary source:
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-11-01/pdf/2023-24283.pdf

### 2025 change

The incoming administration rescinded EO 14110 and ordered agencies to suspend, revise, rescind or exempt actions that conflicted with a new policy of sustaining and enhancing U.S. AI dominance, economic competitiveness and national security. The White House explicitly described the previous approach as a barrier to innovation.

Primary sources:
- https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence/
- https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/01/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-takes-action-to-enhance-americas-ai-leadership/

The U.S. AI Safety Institute was then transformed into the **Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI)**. Commerce called the new center "pro-innovation" and said it would focus on commercial AI, voluntary standards and demonstrable national-security risks.

Primary sources:
- https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2025/06/statement-us-secretary-commerce-howard-lutnick-transforming-us-ai
- https://www.nist.gov/caisi

### Classification

**Regime-level frame reversal — strong, but not a same-actor constraint ratchet.** A change of administration matters. This case proves that the safety architecture was politically reversible and that the same federal machinery could be repurposed around leadership, innovation and security.

It should not be used as evidence that a single administration cynically changed its own principles.

## 3. European Union — important counterexample

The EU AI Act entered into force in August 2024 and created binding obligations, including transparency, documentation and additional requirements for general-purpose models with systemic risk. Those GPAI obligations began applying in August 2025.

Primary sources:
- https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/ai-act-enters-force-2024-08-01_en
- https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/general-purpose-ai-obligations-under-ai-act

At the same time, the EU launched an AI Continent Action Plan centered on competitiveness, investment, AI factories and leadership, while also describing implementation support and simplification of the AI Act.

Primary source:
- https://commission.europa.eu/topics/competitiveness/ai-continent_en

### Classification

**Counterexample / mixed case.** Competitiveness pressure increased, but the EU did not simply discard the binding framework. It retained hard law while trying to reduce implementation friction and increase investment.

This weakens any universal claim that strategic competition automatically dissolves safety constraints.

## 4. Philanthropic funding network — shared infrastructure, not proof of command

The Survival and Flourishing Fund reports roughly $35M in 2025 grantmaking and lists grants from Jaan Tallinn that include:

- Encode AI Corporation — $516,000
- Tarbell Center for AI Journalism — $583,000 plus a matching component
- Institute for AI Policy and Strategy — $272,000 plus a matching component
- multiple AI-safety and institutional organizations

Primary source:
- https://survivalandflourishing.fund/

AI Futures Project states that its funding comes primarily from the Survival and Flourishing Fund and a major individual donor. Its executive director previously worked at OpenAI on governance and scenario planning, and the project produces scenario and policy work such as AI 2027 and AI 2040.

Primary source:
- https://www.aifutures.org/about

### Classification

**Shared funding topology — established. Central control — not established.**

A common philanthropic network can create common vocabulary, professional networks, research priorities and repeated exposure to similar risk frames without any donor directing exact conclusions.

That distinction is essential.

## 5. Journalism — a real diffusion channel with explicit independence safeguards

Tarbell places AI-focused fellows inside major newsrooms including Bloomberg, The Guardian, MIT Technology Review, NBC News, Time, The Verge and others. Its stated goal is for AI coverage to receive urgency comparable to other societal-scale challenges.

Primary sources:
- https://www.tarbellcenter.org/fellowship
- https://www.tarbellcenter.org/about

Tarbell publicly lists major supporters including Coefficient Giving/Open Philanthropy and the Survival and Flourishing Fund. It also states that donors have no editorial control, bars direct frontier-AI corporate donations and says fellows retain editorial autonomy.

Primary sources:
- https://www.tarbellcenter.org/about
- https://www.tarbellcenter.org/fundraising-and-gift-acceptance-policy

### Classification

**Frame-diffusion channel — structurally real. Editorial capture — unsupported.**

The interesting fact is not that journalists are "controlled." It is that a small issue-specialist ecosystem can train, fund and place reporters across many high-status outlets. This can increase the probability that particular questions, concepts and source networks appear repeatedly across otherwise independent newsrooms.

That is a diffusion mechanism, not proof of coordinated messaging.

---

## 6. Policy advocacy — funding overlap with independence barriers

Encode works directly on AI policy and receives support from several philanthropic funders including the Survival and Flourishing Fund and Future of Life Institute. Encode says it refuses funding from frontier-AI companies, foreign governments and executives at frontier AI companies.

Primary sources:
- https://encodeai.org/who-we-are/
- https://encodeai.org/donation/

### Classification

**Network overlap with an explicit anti-capture barrier.**

This is useful precisely because it complicates the story: common funders do not automatically imply frontier-lab control, and some organizations deliberately structure themselves against that form of capture.

## Cross-institution result so far

| Domain | Earlier frame/constraint | Later pressure or architecture | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK government | broad frontier AI safety / catastrophic-risk science | security, crime, national security, growth | strong frame migration |
| U.S. federal government | safety-first federal EO and AI Safety Institute | AI dominance, deregulation, CAISI, voluntary standards | strong regime-level reversal |
| European Union | binding risk-based AI Act | simultaneous competitiveness/investment push | counterexample / hard law retained |
| Philanthropy | grants to research, policy, journalism | cross-domain shared funding network | shared infrastructure, no command proven |
| Journalism | independent newsrooms | specialized fellowships and training across outlets | diffusion channel, capture unsupported |
| Policy advocacy | independent policy organizations | overlapping philanthropic funders | network overlap with anti-capture safeguards |

## Provisional result

Two distinct mechanisms now need to be kept separate:

### A. Constraint migration

A hard rule or safety architecture is replaced by a more flexible mechanism when it collides with capital, competition, deployment, national security or political feasibility.

This appeared in the company audit and in government/institutional cases.

### B. Frame infrastructure

Research, policy advocacy, philanthropy and journalism can share funders, personnel, vocabulary and scenario material. That creates an infrastructure through which a frame can diffuse quickly across institutions without requiring a secret command structure.

The existence of this infrastructure is **not** proof that the frame is false. It can spread true warnings, false warnings, or mixed claims.

---

## Strongest alternative explanation

The same facts are compatible with a non-cynical explanation:

1. frontier AI genuinely became more capable and strategically important;
2. institutions updated rationally;
3. national-security and economic priorities naturally rose;
4. philanthropy funded neglected public-interest work;
5. specialist journalists and policy groups independently converged because they faced the same evidence.

Narrative Watch must therefore test not just whether the same frame appears, but whether actors behave differently when the frame becomes costly to their own interests.

---

## What would weaken the broader hypothesis

- Governments retain or strengthen binding safety rules when those rules impose demonstrable strategic costs.
- Funded organizations repeatedly publish conclusions hostile to their own funders' preferred policy direction.
- Journalists trained through the same ecosystem systematically produce divergent interpretations rather than convergent framing.
- Policy actors support constraints that materially reduce their own access, influence or institutional funding.
- EU-style binding obligations survive strong competitive pressure and are actually enforced rather than hollowed out.

---

## Next test

Do not ask merely whether institutions share language.

Track **costly divergence**:

> When a shared frame implies a decision that materially harms the actor, funder, government, newsroom, laboratory or policy organization carrying that frame, does the actor still follow the principle?

This is a much stronger discriminator between sincere principle, institutional convenience and strategic narrative use.
