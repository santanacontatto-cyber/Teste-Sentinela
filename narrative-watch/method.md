# Method — how this investigation avoids becoming its own narrative

## 1. Unit of analysis

The basic unit is an **event chain**:

`prior position → event → interpretation → proposed response → institutional outcome`

The project does not score intentions directly unless there is explicit evidence. It scores observable changes in language, policy, access, authority, funding and constraints.

## 2. Evidence classes

### E0 — assertion
A claim with no supporting source. Not usable for conclusions.

### E1 — secondary report
Reputable reporting describing an event or statement. Useful, but should be replaced or corroborated with primary material where possible.

### E2 — primary public record
Official policy document, first-party statement, source dataset, recorded testimony, public grant record, legislation, or direct interview.

### E3 — independently corroborated technical/institutional record
Multiple independent sources or a technically documented investigation with enough material to evaluate limitations.

No evidence class, including E3, proves hidden intention by itself.

## 3. Claim labels

Every consequential statement should be mentally classed as one of:

- **FACT** — source directly supports it.
- **INFERENCE** — best explanation drawn from facts.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — proposition being tested.
- **PREDICTION** — future claim locked before outcome.
- **SPECULATION** — possible but currently weakly supported.

A common failure mode is silently promoting INFERENCE → FACT. This project treats that as an error.

## 4. Network evidence

Shared personnel, donors, institutions, vocabulary or conference attendance can establish **overlap**. They do not by themselves establish:

- central coordination,
- common intent,
- fabrication,
- corruption,
- deception.

For stronger coordination claims, require direct communication, shared plans, synchronized action inconsistent with independent behavior, financial control, or comparable evidence.

## 5. Intent evidence

Intent is graded separately:

- **I0:** no evidence of intent.
- **I1:** incentive exists.
- **I2:** actor publicly states a compatible goal.
- **I3:** actor takes costly/repeated actions consistent with that goal.
- **I4:** direct documentary/recorded evidence of the specific intent claimed.

Do not call I1 “proof.” Incentives are everywhere.

## 6. Policy-capture test

For each proposed safety measure, record:

1. **Scope** — who is regulated?
2. **Threshold** — what triggers the rule?
3. **Cost** — who bears delay, disclosure, capital or compliance cost?
4. **Access** — who gains privileged information or institutional access?
5. **Exemptions** — who is excluded?
6. **Symmetry** — does the proponent bear the same rule?
7. **Reversibility** — is the measure temporary or structurally sticky?
8. **Alternative policies** — were less concentrating options considered?

A safety measure can be both genuinely protective and competitively advantageous. Those are not mutually exclusive categories.

## 7. Focusing-event test

To claim that an event opened a policy window, require at least:

- a relevant event becomes publicly salient;
- actors explicitly connect it to policy urgency or materially change their policy language;
- a concrete proposal/action follows.

To claim a **prepared frame**, additionally show that substantially similar interpretation/policy existed before the event.

To claim deliberate exploitation, require evidence beyond temporal sequence.

## 8. Prediction scoring

Each prediction gets one status:

- **HIT** — pre-written criterion satisfied.
- **MISS** — time window closes without criterion being satisfied.
- **PARTIAL / METHOD FAILURE** — event exposes ambiguity in the criterion; counts as a failure of design, not a hit.
- **UNRESOLVED** — source/evidence incomplete before deadline.

No retroactive wording changes.

## 9. Updating confidence

Use qualitative Bayesian discipline rather than fake precision:

- evidence strongly expected under both hypotheses → little update;
- evidence expected under working hypothesis but surprising under alternative → meaningful update;
- evidence expected under alternative but surprising under working hypothesis → downgrade;
- evidence selected after seeing the outcome → discount heavily.

## 10. Adversarial questions

For every apparently supportive event, answer:

- What would a sincere safety actor do differently?
- What would a self-interested incumbent do differently?
- Could both generate the same observable behavior?
- What evidence would distinguish them?
- Did we search equally hard for evidence against our preferred story?

## 11. Public audit rule

Git history is part of the protocol. Wrong predictions remain visible. Corrections receive new commits. If a conclusion changes, record **why** it changed and which evidence caused the update.

The public value of this project is not that Sentinela “called it.” The value is that anyone can inspect what was believed **before** the next event and compare it with what actually happened.
