# Narrative Watch — Mission Interpreter Governance

Date frozen: 2026-09-13

The Identity Anchor Model produced a harder question:

> **Who gets to decide what the mission means when the mission conflicts with growth, deployment, capital or state power?**

This matters because a strong identity statement can still be weak in practice if the same actor who benefits from relaxing it also has final authority to reinterpret it.

## New structural distinction

There are at least three kinds of mission guardian:

1. **financially coupled internal guardian** — formally mission-focused, but its resources or equity value rise with company success;
2. **financially disinterested internal guardian** — has governance authority but is deliberately insulated from company equity upside;
3. **external guardian** — regulator, court, statute, contract or other mechanism able to impose a constraint against company preference.

The hypothesis is not that one type is automatically honest and another corrupt. It is that their incentives and ability to resist pressure are structurally different.

## OpenAI — mission control is real, but economically coupled

OpenAI says the nonprofit Foundation controls the OpenAI Group PBC. The Foundation holds 26% of Group equity, also holds a warrant for additional equity if valuation crosses a milestone, and says it is positioned to be the single largest long-term beneficiary of OpenAI's success. OpenAI explicitly says the structure links Foundation resources to the long-term value created by Group growth.

Most current Foundation directors also serve on the Group board, with limited exceptions and planned separation for additional directors over time.

That means the mission guardian is powerful, but **not financially disinterested**. The structure intentionally aligns mission, long-term incentives and commercial success rather than placing them in separate hands.

Primary source:
- https://openai.com/our-structure/

Classification: **high formal authority, high economic coupling, substantial board overlap.**

This is not proof of capture. It does mean that a future conflict cannot be modeled as `mission guardian versus commercial company` as if they were fully independent actors.

## Anthropic — an explicitly disinterested mission guardian

Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust was designed differently. Anthropic says the Trust is independent, its trustees are financially disinterested, and its Class T stock gives it authority to elect and remove board members, phasing toward a board majority. The purpose is explicitly to insulate governance from short-term commercial and shareholder pressure.

Primary sources:
- https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust
- https://www.anthropic.com/transparency/voluntary-commitments

Classification: **formal mission authority with deliberate financial insulation.**

The Trust is therefore a cleaner test of whether independent mission governance actually changes outcomes when pressure becomes expensive.

## Alphabet / Google — concentrated voting control, no separate mission guardian

Alphabet's own annual reporting says Larry Page and Sergey Brin retain significant voting control through Class B shares and can strongly influence director elections and major corporate transactions. Google's AI Principles are company policy, not an independently enforced mission trust.

Primary sources:
- https://abc.xyz/investor/
- https://ai.google/responsibility/principles/

Classification: **leadership/founder-centered interpretation with no separate AI-mission veto body identified.**

This helps explain how Google could preserve a broad organizational identity while replacing the 2018 application-level prohibitions with the broader 2025 framework.

## Meta — even stronger founder control

Meta's 2026 proxy says Mark Zuckerberg holds roughly 60.8% of total voting power while owning far less than a majority of the economic interest, making Meta a controlled company under Nasdaq rules.

Primary source:
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026025532/meta-20260416.htm

Classification: **very high founder control; no independent AI-mission guardian with comparable authority identified.**

That does not tell us which policies Meta will choose. It tells us who can ultimately define continuity when policies change.

## Interpreter Independence Hypothesis

A constraint should survive material pressure more strongly when two conditions are both true:

1. the constraint is identity-anchored; and
2. the actor empowered to interpret/enforce it does **not** share most of the upside created by relaxing it.

This modifies the earlier model:

`Constraint survival ≈ identity anchor × interpreter independence × external enforceability`

A strong identity with a weakly independent interpreter can preserve the words while changing the operational meaning.

## The OpenAI/Anthropic comparison is now especially valuable

Both companies use public-benefit governance language. But their mission guardians are structurally different:

- OpenAI Foundation: controls Group and directly participates in Group equity upside.
- Anthropic LTBT: explicitly designed to be financially disinterested and to gain board appointment power.

That creates a natural comparative test. If a future high-stakes conflict hits both organizations, the difference in guardian structure may predict how much real self-restraint occurs.

## What would falsify this

The hypothesis is weakened if financially coupled guardians repeatedly impose durable, very large self-costs while financially disinterested guardians repeatedly permit comparable mission-level relaxations.

It is also weakened if independent mission bodies remain irrelevant even when conflicts directly concern the principles they were created to protect.

## Next operational rule

For every future 'principled' decision, Narrative Watch will ask four questions before interpreting motive:

1. Who had final authority?
2. Who appoints or removes that authority?
3. Does that authority financially benefit from relaxing the rule?
4. Could an external actor enforce the opposite outcome?

That should tell us more than another hundred mission statements.
