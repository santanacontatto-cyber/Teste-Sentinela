# Predictions — locked at launch

These predictions are intentionally specific enough to fail. They concern the period **after the commit that creates this file**.

Do not edit the wording after outcomes begin to arrive. Add results beneath each prediction or in `ledger.md`.

## P1 — Incident language will become policy language

**Window:** 30 days.

**Prediction:** At least one new U.S. federal legislative proposal, executive proposal, formal congressional hearing agenda, or agency proposal will explicitly connect frontier/agentic AI incidents or rapid autonomous-capability growth to **mandatory incident reporting, third-party evaluator access, or capability-triggered safety requirements**.

**Counts as a hit only if:**
- the proposal/action is new after this commit; and
- the justification explicitly references agentic/frontier capability risk, recent incidents, recursive self-improvement, or loss-of-control concerns; and
- at least one of the three mechanisms above is concrete rather than rhetorical.

**Disconfirmation:** No qualifying proposal/action appears within 30 days.

**Why it matters:** It tests whether the current focusing event is converted into the policy menu already visible in OpenAI/Amodei proposals.

## P2 — A cross-lab safety coordination structure will become more concrete

**Window:** 45 days.

**Prediction:** At least two frontier AI labs will announce, endorse, or enter a shared mechanism that goes beyond generic principles and includes at least one of: shared pacing commitments, common evaluator access, reciprocal incident reporting, shared deployment thresholds, or a standing joint safety body.

**Does not count:** a joint letter with no operational mechanism.

**Disconfirmation:** No qualifying mechanism appears within 45 days.

**Why it matters:** The current narrative repeatedly identifies competitive racing as the reason unilateral restraint is unstable.

---

## P3 — The same policy will be sold as both restraint and competitiveness

**Window:** 60 days.

**Prediction:** A major frontier lab or senior U.S. policy actor will publicly argue for a concrete safety/pacing measure while also explicitly framing it as compatible with, or necessary for, preserving U.S. technological/geopolitical leadership.

**Counts as a hit only if:** both claims occur in the same proposal, speech, testimony, or official policy document.

**Disconfirmation:** Safety proposals during the window consistently accept strategic/competitive disadvantage rather than pairing restraint with leadership preservation.

**Why it matters:** It tests whether the frame can support both “slow down” and “do not lose the race.”

---

## P4 — Prepared actors will gain formal access

**Window:** 90 days.

**Prediction:** At least one organization or individual already active before 2026-09-13 in frontier-AI evaluation, forecasting or safety policy will receive a **new formal role or privileged access** in a lab/government safety process: embedded evaluator access, formal advisory appointment, contracted evaluation mandate, standing committee seat, or equivalent.

**Counts as a hit only if:** the role/access is new and publicly documented after this commit.

**Disconfirmation:** New safety institutions are formed but rely mainly on actors outside the pre-existing ecosystem, or no such formalization occurs.

**Why it matters:** It tests the network-overlap hypothesis using institutional outcomes rather than rhetoric.

## P5 — The current incidents will be cited repeatedly as policy evidence

**Window:** 60 days.

**Prediction:** The OpenAI/Hugging Face incident, Jacob Coxon's resignation/warning, or closely related recursive-self-improvement concerns will be cited as justification in at least **three distinct policy-facing artifacts** produced after this commit, across at least two categories below:

1. government hearing / official statement,
2. frontier-lab policy proposal,
3. major think-tank / advocacy policy paper,
4. formal international-governance proposal.

**Disconfirmation:** Fewer than three qualifying artifacts appear.

**Why it matters:** It measures whether a technical/public event becomes a reusable policy reference point.

## P6 — Strong test against capture

**Window:** 90 days.

**Prediction:** At least one top frontier lab will support a safety rule that imposes a **material, symmetric cost on itself** — such as a measurable training delay, independent access it cannot veto, deployment restriction, or mandatory disclosure — without obtaining an obvious incumbent-specific exemption.

**This prediction is deliberately hostile to the capture hypothesis.**

**Hit:** weakens the claim that safety policy is mainly a vehicle for incumbent advantage.

**Miss:** does not prove capture, but increases the importance of examining exemptions and asymmetries.

# Structural falsifiers

The broader working model should be downgraded substantially if any two of these occur:

1. A major future AI incident produces a policy response materially outside the pre-existing menu (pacing, evaluator access, incident reporting, compute/governance controls) and that alternative becomes dominant.
2. Frontier labs repeatedly advocate measures that impose larger costs on themselves than on smaller competitors, without seeking compensating exemptions.
3. Actors outside the existing safety/forecasting network consistently gain the key formal roles after focusing events.
4. Policy actors explicitly reject the current crisis frame despite accepting the underlying technical facts, and this rejection becomes institutionally dominant.
5. The predictions above perform no better than chance / vague baseline expectations over multiple cycles.

# Anti-cheating rule

A near miss is a miss unless the pre-written criterion is satisfied. If a criterion turns out to be badly designed, record that as a methodology failure; do not retroactively broaden it.
