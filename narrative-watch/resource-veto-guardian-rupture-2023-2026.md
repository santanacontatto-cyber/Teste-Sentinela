# Narrative Watch — Resource Veto & Guardian Rupture

Date frozen: 2026-09-13

The selector-loop audit produced a harder question:

> **Formal authority is one thing. Can a mission guardian actually keep a costly decision in force when the people who supply the organization’s critical resources revolt?**

## OpenAI 2023 is the clean historical stress test

On 17 November 2023, OpenAI's nonprofit board used its formal authority to remove Sam Altman as CEO, saying it no longer had confidence in him because he had not been consistently candid with the board.

Within four days, the outcome reversed. Nearly all of OpenAI's roughly 700 employees threatened to leave unless Altman returned and the board resigned. Investors and Microsoft also strongly opposed the removal. Altman returned as CEO and a reconstituted initial board took over.

In March 2024, the WilmerHale review summarized by OpenAI concluded that the old board acted within its broad discretion, but had not anticipated that the removal would destabilize the company. The new board expressed full confidence in Altman and added governance mechanisms including strengthened conflict rules, a whistleblower hotline, and additional board committees.

## Result

The event is a strong counterexample to any claim that a closed/self-renewing governance loop is automatically a rubber stamp. The old board was willing to use its authority against the CEO and absorb enormous immediate pressure.

But it also exposes another mechanism:

> **A guardian can possess legal authority and still lack practical authority if the organization’s critical resources can credibly exit.**

Employees, investors, cloud/infrastructure partners, major customers and other resource holders may have no formal mission veto yet still create a de facto veto by making a decision operationally unsustainable.

This is not the same as corruption or capture. It is a structural constraint on governance.

## Guardian-survival distinction

The 2023 episode suggests separating three things:

1. **formal decision authority** — who can legally make the decision;
2. **resource independence** — whether that decision-maker can keep the institution functioning after making it;
3. **guardian survivability** — whether the decision-maker remains in power after imposing the costly constraint.

A mission guardian can score high on (1) and low on (2) and (3).

## Why this changes the Narrative Watch model

Until now the model emphasized selector closure and mission interpretation. That is incomplete.

A more useful equation is:

`effective constraint power ≈ formal authority × selector independence × resource independence × external enforceability`

If any factor approaches zero, a formally strong safeguard may become fragile under real conflict.

## Important counterweight

The OpenAI 2023 board crisis does not establish that employees or investors were opposing AI safety. The later review said the firing was not based on product safety, development speed, finances, or similar issues; it was described as a breakdown of trust and governance. Former board member Helen Toner later disputed parts of the surrounding narrative and described serious management concerns. Narrative Watch therefore uses the episode as a **governance stress test**, not as proof of a safety-versus-profit conflict.

## New discriminator

For each future mission/safety conflict, track:

- Who has formal authority?
- Which critical resources can exit or resist?
- How credible is that exit threat?
- Does the guardian control enough independent resources to survive the decision?
- Does the principle survive while the guardian is replaced?
- Does the guardian survive while the principle is weakened?
- Does external law/court/regulation stabilize the decision when internal resource pressure rises?

## Current conclusion

The strongest historical lesson is not merely that principles can migrate.

It is:

> **Formal mission governance can be real and sincere while still being bounded by a resource veto that becomes visible only when the guardian actually uses its power.**

This gives Narrative Watch another observable variable: not just who selects the guardian, but who can make the guardian's decision impossible to sustain.
